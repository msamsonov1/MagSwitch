Electromechanical & Magnetodynamic Core Formulation

Rigorous mathematical description of dynamic coil inductance variation during contact separation, molten bridge surface tension, and electromagnetic weld-severance pulses.

1. Dynamic Coil Inductance & Reluctance Inversion

In an electromechanical solenoid relay, the total coil inductance $L(x)$ is a direct reciprocal function of the magnetic circuit reluctance $\mathcal{R}(x)$. As the armature displaces across physical gap $x(t)$, magnetic flux path length expands:

$$L(x) = \frac{N^2}{\mathcal{R}_{\text{core}} + \mathcal{R}_{\text{gap}}(x)} = \frac{\mu_0 A_{\text{pole}} N^2}{2 x(t) + \frac{\mu_0}{\mu_r} l_{\text{core}}}$$

The mechanical opening equation relates opening spring constant $k_s$, damping coefficient $c$, and residual magnetic holding force $F_{\text{mag}}$:

$$m \frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} + c \frac{dx}{dt} + k_s (x_0 - x) = -F_{\text{mag}}(i, x) = -\frac{1}{2} i_{\text{coil}}^2 \frac{\partial L}{\partial x}$$

During commanded contact separation, $i_{\text{coil}} \to 0$, causing $x(t)$ to rapidly expand from $0 \to x_{\text{max}}$, collapsing $L(x)$ from its closed peak $L_{\text{closed}}$ to open floor $L_{\text{open}}$.

2. Holm Molten Metal Bridge & Micro-Weld Formation

In high-current DC disconnectors (e.g., 180A–350A solar wings), as metallic contacts begin separating, the constriction resistance $R_c$ increases asymptotically according to Holm’s contact theory:

$$R_c(t) = \frac{\rho_{\text{metal}}}{2 a(t)} = \frac{\rho_{\text{metal}}}{2} \sqrt{\frac{\pi H}{F_{\text{contact}}(t)}}$$

Joule heating concentrates intensely at microscopic asperities. When super-temperature $\theta_{\text{max}}$ exceeds the alloy melting point $T_{\text{melt}}$ (1,356 K for Ag-SnO$_2$ matrix):

$$U_c^2 = 8 \int_0^{\theta_{\text{max}}} \rho(T) \cdot \lambda(T) \, dT \quad \implies \quad F_{\text{weld}} \approx \sigma_{\text{yield}} \cdot \pi r_{\text{bridge}}^2$$

If $F_{\text{weld}} \ge F_{\text{spring}}$, armature motion stalls completely at gap $x \approx 0.02 - 0.08\text{ mm}$. The coil inductance drop ceases instantly, pinning the trajectory in the dangerous hazard zone of the $(F, L)$ phase space.

3. High-Frequency $(F, L)$ State Observer Sieve

Rather than relying on sluggish optical encoders or vulnerable Hall sensors, MagSwitch continuously senses coil back-EMF and injects a 50 kHz micro-probe carrier to calculate instantaneous $\hat{L}(t)$ and inferred mechanical force $\hat{F}(t)$:

$$v_{\text{sense}}(t) = R_{\text{coil}} i(t) + L(x)\frac{di}{dt} + i(t)\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}\right)\frac{dx}{dt}$$

The autonomous sentry computes the weld anomaly indicator $\Psi_{\text{weld}}(t)$ defined over transit window $\Delta \tau_{\text{transit}} \in [10, 85] \, \mu\text{s}$:

$$\Psi_{\text{weld}}(t) = \int_{t_0}^{t_0+\Delta \tau} \left| \frac{dL}{dt} \right|^{-1} \cdot \Theta\left(F_{\text{command}} - F_{\text{threshold}}\right) \, dt > \Gamma_{\text{crit}}$$

Where $\Theta(\cdot)$ is the Heaviside step function. If $\Psi_{\text{weld}}$ trips threshold $\Gamma_{\text{crit}}$ within 45 microseconds of trip command, a micro-weld is unequivocally confirmed.

4. Capacitive Discharge Counter-Flux Pulse Shock

$$F_{\text{pulse}}(t) = \frac{1}{2} i_{\text{pulse}}^2(t) \frac{d M_{12}}{dx} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{V_0}{\omega L} e^{-\alpha t} \sin(\omega t) \right]^2 \frac{\mu_0 N_{\text{main}} N_{\text{aux}} A}{x^2}$$

The resultant transient peak force ($> 320 \text{ N}$ for $12 \, \mu\text{s}$) exceeds the mechanical shear yield strength of the frozen metallic neck:

$$\int_0^{\tau_p} F_{\text{pulse}}(t) \, dt \gg \mathcal{J}_{\text{fracture}} = \sqrt{2 E \gamma_s \cdot \pi r_{\text{bridge}}^3}$$
